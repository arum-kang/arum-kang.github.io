Research

Rhetorical Questions & Discourse

A central thread in my work concerns how rhetorical questions function as discourse moves rather than information-seeking acts. I analyze how they update discourse commitments, project inferential obligations, and interact with polarity focus and exclamative interpretation. Recent work investigates how commonsense reasoning and dialogue structure constrain rhetorical question interpretation, combining formal analysis with corpus evidence and LLM evaluation.

Key phenomena include verum focus, polarity reversals, negative politeness, and the prosodic marking of rhetorical vs. information-seeking questions in Korean.

Definiteness & Anaphora in Articleless Languages

My work on definiteness examines how languages without articles — specifically Korean and Mandarin Chinese — encode and track discourse-familiar referents. I investigate how bare nominals, demonstratives, and scalar particles serve the functions that definite articles perform in languages like English, using tools from dynamic semantics, situation semantics, and type-shifting theory.

A main focus is donkey anaphora: how universal and existential readings arise in conditionals and relative clauses across typologically diverse languages. I also study weak and strong definite distinctions, domain restriction, and the role of classifier structures in East Asian languages.

Mood, Modality & Subjunctive

I investigate the semantics of mood selection in Korean embedded environments, focusing on the interaction between attitude predicates, complementizer choice, and (non)veridicality. Korean complementizers -nka, -nchi, -lka, and -lchi impose fine-grained constraints on embedding predicates, tracking inquisitiveness, nonveridicality, and epistemic uncertainty.

Related work examines modal concord, epistemic modality, disjunction, and free choice. I am also interested in factive alternation and how factivity interacts with modal spread and expletive negation.

Korean Sign Language Semantics

I study the semantics and pragmatics of Korean Sign Language (KSL), focusing on definiteness marking and spatial grammar. KSL uses pointing signs (index) in both weak definite functions — tracking discourse-introduced referents — and strong definite functions — identifying maximally salient entities. I analyze these as NPLOC and NP-IXTHIS constructions respectively, with implications for the typology of definiteness across modalities.

Current work with Youngju Choi examines scalar adjectives in KSL, including the scalarity of STRONG and its interaction with verum focus and polarity.

Corpus Linguistics & Computational Semantics

I use corpus data and computational methods to study semantic and pragmatic phenomena — including large-scale corpus analysis of mood selection and complementizer distribution, development of Korean CommitmentBank resources, and evaluation of large language models on epistemic and pragmatic inference tasks in Korean.
