Research

Interrogative Semantics, Modality & Inference

My research investigates the interaction of modality, speaker inference, and question semantics in Korean, with particular focus on modalized questions, disjunction, inquisitiveness, and nonveridical equilibrium. Recent work extends this approach to mood selection, embedded questions, and concessive constructions.

Selected papers
Kang, Arum and Suwon Yoon. 2020. From Inquisitive Disjunction to Nonveridical Equilibrium: Modalized Questions in Korean. Linguistics 58(1): 207–244.
Kang, Arum and Suwon Yoon. 2022. Two Types of Subjunctive in Korean: Interaction between Inquisitiveness and Nonveridicality. Empirical Issues in Syntax and Semantics 14: 113–133. slides proceedings
Supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (2022–2023)

Definiteness, Quantification & Reference

My research on definiteness examines how articleless languages encode reference and anaphora without overt articles, with particular attention to bare nominals, demonstratives, and domain restriction. A central topic concerns the Korean marker ku, analyzed as a domain restrictor. Recent work extends this approach to donkey anaphora, quantification, and coreference.

Selected papers
Kang, Arum. 2021. Marking Definiteness in an Articleless Language: The Role of the Domain Restrictor KU in Korean. Language and Linguistics 22(2): 301–336.
Park, Myung-Kwan and Arum Kang. 2024. Anaphoric Definites without a Determiner: Korean vs. Chinese. Journal of Cognitive Science 25(1): 67–124.
Supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (2024–2025)

Rhetorical Questions & Discourse Inference

This line of research addresses rhetorical questions as discourse moves that update speaker commitments and shape discourse expectations, examining polarity focus, verum effects, and commonsense inference in Korean.

Selected papers
Kang, Arum. 2024. A study on rhetorical questions in Korean: From the view of Verum Focus. 언어와 언어학 105: 1–30.
Supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (2025–2027)

Additional Research Directions

Related work extends these questions to Korean Sign Language semantics — focusing on definiteness, spatial reference, and scalarity in KSL — and to corpus-based and computational approaches to meaning, including LLM evaluation of epistemic inference.
