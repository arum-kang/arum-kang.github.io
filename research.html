Research

Rhetorical Questions & Discourse

A central thread in my work concerns how rhetorical questions function as discourse moves rather than information-seeking acts. I analyze how they update discourse commitments, project inferential obligations, and interact with polarity focus and exclamative interpretation. More recently, I have been investigating how commonsense reasoning and dialogue structure constrain rhetorical question interpretation, combining formal semantic analysis with corpus evidence and LLM evaluation.

Key phenomena include verum focus, polarity reversals, negative politeness, and the prosodic marking of rhetorical vs. information-seeking questions in Korean.

Related: 강아름 (2025), 언어연구 41(1) · 강아름 (2024), 언어와 언어학 105 · Ahn & Kang (2020)

Definiteness & Anaphora in Articleless Languages

My work on definiteness examines how languages without articles — specifically Korean and Mandarin Chinese — encode and track discourse-familiar referents. I investigate how bare nominals, demonstratives, and scalar particles serve the functions that definite articles perform in languages like English, using tools from dynamic semantics, situation semantics, and type-shifting theory.

A main focus is donkey anaphora: how universal and existential readings arise in conditionals and relative clauses across typologically diverse languages. I also study weak and strong definite distinctions, domain restriction, and the role of classifier structures in East Asian languages.

Related: Park & Kang (2024), Journal of Cognitive Science · Kang (2021), Language and Linguistics · Kang (2025, ms.)
Supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (신진연구지원사업, 2024–2025)

Mood, Modality & Subjunctive

I investigate the semantics of mood selection in Korean embedded environments, focusing on the interaction between attitude predicates, complementizer choice, and (non)veridicality. Korean presents a particularly rich testing ground: the complementizers -nka, -nchi, -lka, and -lchi impose fine-grained constraints on what types of embedding predicates they combine with, tracking inquisitiveness, nonveridicality, and epistemic uncertainty.

Related work examines modal concord, epistemic modality, disjunction, and the semantics of free choice. I am also interested in factive alternation and how factivity interacts with modal spread and expletive negation.

Related: Kang & Yoon (2020), Linguistics 58(1) · 강아름 & 송상헌 (2025), 언어 50(3) · 강아름 (2025), 언어학 101

Korean Sign Language Semantics

I study the semantics and pragmatics of Korean Sign Language (KSL), focusing on definiteness marking and spatial grammar. KSL uses pointing signs (index) in both weak definite functions — tracking discourse-introduced referents — and strong definite functions — identifying maximally salient or unique entities. I analyze these as corresponding to NPLOC and NP-IXTHIS constructions respectively, with implications for the typology of definiteness across modalities.

Current work with Youngju Choi examines scalar adjectives in KSL, including the scalarity of the sign STRONG and its interaction with verum focus and polarity.

Related: Kang, Choi & Kim (2025), WCCFL 43 Proceedings · Kang & Choi (2025), WECOL Proceedings
Supported by the National Research Foundation of Korea (공동연구지원사업, 2024–2027)

Corpus Linguistics & Computational Semantics

I have a growing interest in using corpus data and computational methods to study semantic and pragmatic phenomena. This includes large-scale corpus analysis of mood selection and complementizer distribution, development of Korean CommitmentBank resources for factivity and veridicality, and evaluation of large language models on epistemic and pragmatic inference tasks.

Ongoing work examines how well current LLMs capture fine-grained distinctions in Korean modal constructions and periphrastic structures, and whether corpus surprisal measures track theoretical predictions about embedding constraints.

Related: Lee, Song & Kang (2025), PACLIC 2025 · Shin, Kang & Song (2022), 언어학 연구 65
