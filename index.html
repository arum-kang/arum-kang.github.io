Research Profile

My research investigates how meaning, discourse structure, and speaker inference are encoded in Korean and cross-linguistic data. I work primarily in formal semantics and pragmatics, with additional corpus-based approaches to discourse reasoning, reference, and rhetoricality.

Current research focuses on modality and alternative semantics, definiteness and coreference in articleless languages, rhetorical questions and discourse inference, and semantic structure in Korean Sign Language.

I also use corpus annotation and language-model evaluation as methodological tools for investigating semantic and pragmatic structure in natural language.

Ph.D. in Linguistics, University of Chicago (2015).

Research Areas

Modality & Alternative Semantics Nonveridicality, inquisitive disjunction, ignorance inference, and modalized questions in Korean.
Definiteness & Reference Definiteness, domain restriction, and discourse reference in articleless languages, especially Korean and Mandarin Chinese.
Rhetorical Questions & Discourse Inference Speaker bias, polarity focus, rhetoricality, and discourse-governance effects in Korean rhetorical questions.
Korean Sign Language Semantics Definiteness, spatial reference, and discourse structure in Korean Sign Language.
Corpus Semantics & Language Modeling Corpus-based analyses of rhetoricality, concessive expressions, and semantic structure using embedding models and language-model evaluation.
