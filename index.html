Research Profile

My research investigates how modality, speaker uncertainty, and discourse inference are encoded in Korean and cross-linguistic data. I work primarily on modalized questions, disjunction, definiteness, and rhetorical meaning in Korean, combining formal semantic analysis with corpus-based approaches.

A central strand of my work examines how Korean disjunction markers interact with modality, ignorance inference, exclusivity, and unconditionality across discourse contexts. This research explores modalized questions, nonveridicality, and speaker-oriented inference in Korean semantics and pragmatics.

Another major line of research concerns definiteness and discourse reference in articleless languages, especially Korean and Mandarin Chinese, including domain restriction, anaphora, and coreference interpretation.

More recent work extends these questions to rhetoricality, discourse structure, Korean Sign Language semantics, and corpus-based analyses of semantic and pragmatic reasoning.

Ph.D. in Linguistics, University of Chicago (2015).

Research Areas

Questions, Modality & Inference Modalized questions, disjunction, epistemic uncertainty, ignorance inference, and speaker-oriented meaning in Korean semantics and pragmatics.
Definiteness & Reference Definiteness, domain restriction, anaphora, and discourse reference in articleless languages, especially Korean and Mandarin Chinese.
Rhetorical Questions & Discourse Inference Speaker bias, polarity focus, rhetoricality, and discourse-structuring effects in Korean rhetorical questions.
Korean Sign Language Semantics Definiteness, spatial reference, scalarity, and discourse structure in Korean Sign Language.
Corpus-based Semantic Analysis Corpus-based analyses of modality, rhetoricality, concessive expressions, and semantic structure using annotation and computational methods.
